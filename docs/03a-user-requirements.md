# StumpRider user requirements

What the people who would use, make, hand out or promote StumpRider need, written from their side. Product requirements that meet these needs are in SMR-PRS-001 (`03-requirements.md`) and the links are in the traceability matrix SMR-RTM-001 (`03b-traceability.md`). The needs are drawn from the users and stakeholders in SMR-PRB-001 Tables 3 and 4; none has yet been confirmed with a co-design partner, which is the first job of TRL 4. Method: STANDARDS section 22.

> **Safety:** UR-001, UR-003 and UR-011 are the reason the tool exists: nobody is sent into the water, the canoe is never put at risk, and the tool is never a reason to keep children on boats.

Table 1. User requirements.

| ID | Category | Title | Need | Priority | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| UR-001 | Performance | Free the net from the canoe | When a net snags on a drowned tree, the crew can free it from the canoe, so nobody (least of all a child) is sent to dive for it | High | Canoe crews and boat owners; child-protection NGOs (PRB Table 3) |
| UR-002 | Performance | Reach the snag | The tool reaches snags from just below the surface to the depths where nets are set among the trees | High | Canoe crews (PRB, operating environment) |
| UR-003 | Safety | Never risk the canoe | Working a snag never heels the canoe enough to unsettle the crew or capsize it, even when the operator pulls hard | High | Canoe crews; fisheries officers (PRB Table 3) |
| UR-004 | Performance | Keep the net whole | Freeing the net does not tear more than a few meshes, so it can go straight back in the water | Medium | Boat owners (PRB Table 3) |
| UR-005 | Usability | Quick | Freeing a snag takes a few minutes, faster than sending someone down | Medium | Boat owners (PRB assumptions) |
| UR-006 | Usability | Any net | The tool goes on any net line or headrope the crew uses, without cutting or untying it | Medium | Canoe crews |
| UR-007 | Logistics | Stows in a canoe | What is carried to the canoe is short and light enough to stow among the nets and carry from the landing | Medium | Canoe crews |
| UR-008 | Environment and durability | Survives the canoe bottom | The tool still works after months lying wet in the canoe, in sun and rain | Medium | Canoe crews (PRB, operating environment) |
| UR-009 | Maintenance | Made and mended locally | A village smith or carpenter can make it, and mend it, from drawings with hand tools, a small welder and local stock in about a day | High | Village blacksmiths and carpenters (PRB Table 3) |
| UR-010 | Cost | Affordable | Boat owners and NGOs can afford a kit for every canoe that sets nets among trees | High | Boat owners; child-protection NGOs (PRB constraints) |
| UR-011 | Regulatory | Handed out with protection work | The kit reaches canoes only through child-protection and fisheries partners, with its limits and the rule that nobody enters the water stated on it, and never as a reason to keep children on boats | High | Child-protection NGOs; fisheries officers and fisher associations (PRB Table 4; SMR-DDR-001 D9) |

Categories: deployment and assembly, performance, usability, safety, environment and durability, logistics, maintenance, cost, regulatory. Priorities: High, Medium, Low.
